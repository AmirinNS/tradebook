## What Gets Measured Gets Managed

In trading, consistency rarely stems from a secret indicator or prediction model. Instead, sustainable performance is built through disciplined record-keeping, iterative review, and objective statistical feedback. A trade journal is the primary tool for auditing your decision-making process.

Without a journal, traders remember their wins vividly while conveniently forgetting or rationalizing losses, falling prey to confirmation bias and emotional drift.

---

## What Every Trade Entry Must Capture

A thorough journal entry records both objective numbers and qualitative intent:

1. **Trade Specifics**: Date, symbol, trade side (buy/sell), quantity, execution price, and total fees.
2. **Context and Setup Thesis**: Why was the trade initiated? (e.g., breakout above 50-day SMA, oversold RSI bounce, earnings catalyst).
3. **Pre-Planned Parameters**: What was the initial invalidation price (stop-loss) and expected risk/reward target before order entry?
4. **Execution Quality**: Did you adhere to your initial rule parameters, or did fear/FOMO dictate an impulsive deviation?

## Key Performance Metrics Explained

Tradebook computes three foundational statistics across your closed trades:

### 1. Win Rate
```
Win Rate = (Winning Trades / Total Closed Trades) x 100%
```
While intuitive, win rate in isolation is misleading. A trader with an 80% win rate can still be unprofitable if their few losses are four times larger than their average win.

### 2. Profit Factor
```
Profit Factor = Gross Realized Profits / Gross Realized Losses
```
Profit factor compares total money won against total money lost:
- **Below 1.0**: The system loses money.
- **1.0 to 1.5**: Modestly profitable, but vulnerable to fee slippage.
- **1.6 to 2.0+**: Robust trading system with healthy positive expectancy.
*(If a portfolio has zero losses, profit factor is undefined / N/A).*

### 3. Win/Loss Ratio (Expectancy)
Expectancy combines your win rate with your average reward-to-risk ratio:

```
Expectancy = (Win Rate x Average Win) - (Loss Rate x Average Loss)
```

Even a 40% win rate generates substantial wealth if average winners are 2.5 times larger than average losers.

## Overcoming Psychological Drift

Reviewing your journal on a weekly or monthly cadence reveals recurring behavioral patterns:
- Cutting winning positions too early out of anxiety while allowing losing positions to run.
- Revenge trading immediately following a stop-out.
- Sizing positions too aggressively during winning streaks.

By confronting your objective trade statistics in the ledger, you eliminate guesswork and build an evidence-based edge.
