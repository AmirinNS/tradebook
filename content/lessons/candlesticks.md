## The Anatomy of Price Action

A candlestick chart packs four critical pieces of trading information into a single visual bar: the **Open**, **High**, **Low**, and **Close** (OHLC) over a specific timeframe (such as a 1-day session).

```
        High
         |        Upper Wick / Shadow
    +---------+
    |         |   Real Body: Range between Open and Close
    +---------+
         |        Lower Wick / Shadow
        Low
```

Each candle consists of two components:

1. **The Real Body**: The rectangular section between the opening price and closing price.
   - If the close is higher than the open, the candle represents a **gain** (a bullish session).
   - If the close is lower than the open, the candle represents a **loss** (a bearish session).
2. **Wicks (Shadows)**: The thin vertical lines extending above and below the body.
   - The top of the upper wick represents the highest price reached during the session.
   - The bottom of the lower wick represents the lowest price reached during the session.

---

## What Wicks Reveal About Market Sentiment

While the body highlights the net outcome between buyers and sellers, wicks reveal the battle that occurred intra-session:

- **Long Upper Wick**: Buyers attempted to drive the price upward, but sellers pushed prices back down before the close. This indicates **rejection of higher prices**.
- **Long Lower Wick**: Sellers aggressively pushed prices downward, but buyers entered and absorbed the supply, driving the price back up. This indicates **demand or support**.
- **Narrow Body with Equal Wicks (Doji)**: Reflects equilibrium or indecision between supply and demand over that interval.

## Context Trumps Isolated Patterns

Beginner traders often search for isolated single-candle patterns—such as hammers, shooting stars, or engulfing candles—expecting immediate predictive certainty. In reality, candlestick patterns have minimal predictive value when viewed in isolation.

Reliable chart analysis requires **market context**:

1. **Trend Direction**: A hammer candle at the bottom of an extended multi-week decline carries different significance than one appearing in the middle of a choppy sideways range.
2. **Volume Confirmation**: Candle formations accompanied by above-average trading volume indicate institutional participation, whereas low-volume moves are prone to whipsaws.
3. **Support and Resistance**: Rejection wicks occurring directly at key moving averages (such as the 50-day or 200-day SMA) or prior horizontal support levels are far more informative than candles formed in empty space.
